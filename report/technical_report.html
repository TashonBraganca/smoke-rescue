Smoke Rescue

Two quadruped robots search a burning, smoke-filled house with OpenCV 5 perception, live visual SLAM between the robots, and agents that act only on cited OpenCV evidence. OpenCV AI Competition 2026 technical report, 25 October 2026.
Team: names and roles to be filled in by the team. Repository branch overnight-25oct.

1. Problem

A search team in a burning house loses sight to smoke and time to heat, and two searchers who cannot share what they saw search the same rooms twice. We built a simulation of that search with the constraints a real robot team would have: each robot sees only through its own RGB-D camera, thermal camera and planar LiDAR; nobody gets a floor plan; the radio is lossy and short; and every result is scored against ground truth that no robot-side code can read.

The system has to answer three questions live: where am I and where is my partner (visual SLAM), where have we already looked (shared room records over the radio), and is that a person (OpenCV 5 dnn with YOLO11m, never a heat blob alone).

2. Architecture

Figure 1. Unity 6 HDRP simulates the house, fire, smoke and sensors. A C# brain per robot maps, plans and keeps the robot safe at 60 Hz. A Python service (FastAPI) runs the OpenCV 5 perception, place recognition and the 3D map, and hosts the GPT agents and the incident commander. The same service runs on AWS Fargate. Only the evaluator reads ground truth (Unity SmokeRescue.Oracle assembly and oracle/ in each recording).

4. Live visual SLAM between two robots

Both robots launch from pads at the one front door. The pads give a shared frame, but a pad placement error turns a robot's whole map: in seed 934 robot B's true heading was 2.39 degrees off its pad. Over a 15 m house that misplaces B's far walls by about 0.6 m, enough to put a door in a wall.

Each robot stores ORB keyframes with depth and broadcasts them over the radio (7.6 KB each, under the 8 KB interface limit). A robot compares a partner keyframe only with its own views near where the shared-frame prior says the partner stood (3 m, 60 degrees). A match must pass RANSAC with 10 or more inliers. It is then checked a second way: the partner's LiDAR sweep from that moment is placed on the robot's own LiDAR map. Two matches that agree within 1 degree and 0.15 m, taken at least 1 m apart, refine the link, bounded to 5 degrees and 0.5 m. The same matcher against the robot's own views 20 s or older measures drift (loop closure) and applies a correction only if the LiDAR fit does not get worse.

5. Autonomy: brain, shared context and agents

Robot brain (C#, 60 Hz, one per robot)

Occupancy grid from LiDAR and depth; gyro heading corrected by LiDAR scan matching. Room memory: a robot steps inside each room, marks it searched, and never returns to it. Door claims stop both robots taking the same doorway. Fire is handled in code with no agent override: hot air marks a room on fire; the brain sprays measured fire from 2 m or less (it used to fire from up to 4.6 m and the water landed short), then walks through and looks. When nothing reachable is left, both robots walk back out to their pads. Every constant has a written reason in config/brain.json, and no constant describes a particular house.

Distributed context (radio v10)

Map layers, room records, door claims, person confirmations and ORB keyframes travel over a lossy, range-limited radio. Partner data is converted through the measured frame link, never through ground truth.

Agents (GPT via Azure, tool use)

One agent per robot is woken by brain events (a survivor candidate, no frontier left, a goal reached) or every 20 s. It sees only its robot's own pose, offered frontiers, LiDAR summary and radio inbox. It calls the OpenCV tools above and must cite a matching evidence id to act. Python guardrails then check the evidence type and position, allow a confirmation only within 4 m, and refuse invented frontier ids. The C# brain applies a second check (indoors by its own evidence, heat, already leaving) and the refusal reason goes back to the model. An incident commander relays radio reports and approves risky actions. Every step is in agent_trace.jsonl and the robot's decisions.jsonl.

6. Cloud: AWS

CloudFormation stacks in us-east-1 (deploy/aws): the agent and OpenCV 5 API on ECS Fargate ARM64 (1 vCPU, 2 GB) behind an ALB with a WAF rate limit of 2,000 requests per 5 minutes per IP; a Batch job for the offline SLAM container; and budget alarms. The account was not yet verified for CloudFront on 25 October, so the read-only OpenCV routes (/health, /vision/*, /perceive/people, /place/*/match) are open on the ALB. The model routes still require a secret origin header and return 403 without it, so nobody outside can spend the model budget.

What failed, and what we did

  • No run so far ends with both robots outside. On 25 Oct we found the cause: once inside, a robot locks the floor at its way in so it never wanders back out, and the end-of-search walk out planned against that lock, so no route existed at any fire margin. The fix makes that walk out plan to the start pad with the lock lifted; a regression test fails without it and passes with it (logic suite 274/274). Live confirmation: run in progress.
  • Under memory pressure the place service timed out (68 and 69 "Operation aborted" errors for robots A and B in the 25 Oct 11:25 run), and that run found 4 of 5 people with 82 % floor. Perception latency depends on the host.
  • Room memory measured headless on 12 seeded houses: 79/110 rooms entered without it, 80/110 with it, 82/110 with door claims added; floor 76.8, 75.4 and 78.6 %. A small gain, not a solved problem.
  • Recording is slow: about 5 simulated seconds per wall minute with live perception, so each full mission takes about two hours.

9. Responsible operation

  • Robots never read ground truth: Unity assembly definitions and a pytest check keep robot code away from the oracle.
  • Agents cannot act without OpenCV evidence; risky actions need commander approval; refusals are logged and fed back.
  • Cloud spend is capped by budget alarms, the WAF rate limit, and model routes that are closed to the public.
  • This is a simulation. The fire model is an approximation for demonstration, not a validated fire-engineering solver (config/fire.json says so). No result here claims real-world rescue performance.
