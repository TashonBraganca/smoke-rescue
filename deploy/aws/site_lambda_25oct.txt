What it does: one Python 3.12 function on arm64 (Graviton), 256 MB, 70 s timeout.
- GET /, /app.js, /style.css, /runs/... from S3. Files over 5 MB (the 20 MB walkthrough.mp4) get a 302 to a presigned

- GET /api/health, POST /api/vision/<tool>, POST /api/perceive/people go to the ALB without any origin header. Every
  other /api path returns 404 from the function; even if one slipped through, the ALB would answer 403 without the header.

Cost bound, and what differs from the brief: a reserved concurrency cap could not be set. This account's Lambda limit is
10 concurrent executions in total, and Lambda refuses any reservation that leaves fewer than 10 unreserved. That account
limit of 10 is the effective cap: at most 10 x 256 MB running, about 2.9 USD a day if saturated around the clock.
The template takes ReservedConcurrency (e.g. 5) once a quota increase lands. The WAF on the ALB (2000 requests per 5 min
per source IP) now sees the function's IPs, not the judges', so that limit is shared by all site visitors.
