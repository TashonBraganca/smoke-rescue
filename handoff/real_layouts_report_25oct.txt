Selection rule
  All 35,126 file names, sorted, then shuffled with random.Random(20261025). A house is a candidate if it has 6 to 15
  rooms after merging rooms that SUNCG lists twice with the same box (e.g. Kitchen + Dining_Room): 17,084 candidates.
  Candidates are converted in shuffled order until 100 pass or the list ends. The list ended at 39.

Conversion (tools/bench/real_layouts.py; every constant has a *_REASON)
  1. 0.25 m grid (the fire grid) over the house box, x -> x, HouseExpo y -> our z, origin moved to (0, 0).
  2. Each cell takes the smallest room box that contains its centre.
  3. A room is kept as the bounding rectangle of its cells if they fill >= 90 % of it. Neighbours that overlap by one
     0.25 m strip (both SUNCG boxes include the wall) are trimmed. The larger room gives up the strip. Deeper overlap is a
     rejection.
  4. Area covered by no room is split into rectangles. A rectangle becomes a corridor if >= 50 % of it is real floor in
     the HouseExpo outline. Otherwise the house is rejected (17 of 39 houses carry such filler corridors).
  5. Doors: HouseExpo has none, so I read them from the outline. For each pair of rooms sharing >= 1.6 m of wall I take the
     longest run where floor is free 0.20 m on both sides of the wall line. A run >= 0.6 m becomes one door.
  6. Entrance, pads, furniture, people and fire origin all come from the existing generator: the make_doors entrance rule
     (corridor, else living room, on an exterior side), random_layout.place_furniture, random_layout.people, and the same
     fire draw as random_layout.generate. Then random_layout.validate runs, with one change: its 7..12 room-count check is
     replaced by 6..15 plus a duplicate-id check (VALIDATE_IGNORED_REASON).
  Label map: Bedroom/Child_Room/Guest_Room/Room -> bedroom, Living/Dining -> living, Kitchen, Bathroom/Toilet -> bathroom,
  Office/Gym -> study, Hallway/Hall/Entryway/Lobby -> corridor, Storage/Wardrobe -> closet, Boiler_room/Garage -> laundry.
  Houses with Balcony, Terrace, Loggia, Aeration or elevators are rejected.

  walls they do not have. One door per room pair, so a second real opening between the same two rooms is lost.

Why only 39
  Our format and HouseBuilder only build rectangular rooms that tile a rectangular footprint, with doors of at most 1 m.
  Most real houses have an L-shaped room, a non-rectangular outline or a balcony. I tested a 0.80 rectangle-fill threshold
  and it still gives 39: the rejections move from "not rectangular" to "overlap". Reaching 100 would need a format change
  (non-rectangular rooms or footprints, wide openings), which belongs to docs/INTERFACES.md and to you. Selection bias:
  the 39 are the boxy subset of HouseExpo, mostly grid-like houses. They are real layouts, but not a random sample of
  real houses.

Published comparison
  Explore-Bench (Xu et al., ICRA 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11931, code github.com/efc-robot/Explore-Bench) gives
  frontier baselines (nearest-frontier "Cost", RRT "Sample", potential field) and a DRL policy, single- and multi-robot.
  Its maps are its own scenes (loop, corridor, rooms-type layouts and real testbed rooms), not HouseExpo. Its metrics are
  2D occupancy-grid ones: explored area over time and time or path length to finish. I found no table of Explore-Bench
  numbers on HouseExpo maps. HouseExpo's own paper (Li et al., IROS 2019, arXiv 1903.09845) uses its maps for learned
  exploration in its PseudoSLAM 2D simulator. I did not verify, within this budget, whether it reports frontier numbers
  that could be reused, so treat that as open.
  Where our metrics match and where they do not:
   - Coverage: theirs is the share of free grid cells seen by a 2D laser (a range-limited, 360 deg or wide-FOV ray cast in
     an occupancy grid). Ours is usable floor (off tall furniture, 0.125 m from walls) in the union of the robots'
     own_searched masks from a forward RGB-D camera in smoke. Same idea (seen floor / floor), different sensor model and
     denominator. Not like for like.
   - Time: their curves are coverage over simulator steps or seconds of a point/differential robot. Our result.json keeps
     only the final masks, so our T90 is a swept-floor proxy (2 m radius) normalised to the best full run, not seen area.
     Not comparable.
   - Maps: even on HouseExpo, ours are a filtered, rectangularised subset with 1 m doors, furniture and fire. They are not
     their maps.
   - Task: theirs is pure mapping; ours stops for people and fire and has a 480 s mission limit.
  Conclusion: no like-for-like comparison exists today. The closest honest one would run Explore-Bench's nearest-frontier
  baseline in its grid simulator on our 39 converted plans, rasterised, and compare coverage-over-time curves with ours.
  That needs coverage-over-time logging in result.json, which we do not have.

confirmations in either variant. Team overlap 65.6 %.

Verdict: on real house layouts, two robots that share context find more people than one, not only sooner: 99.0 % against
92.3 %, a difference the seeded houses did not show. They also enter more rooms and cover more floor. The cost is the
same as in the main benchmark: both robots get out in 60 % of team missions against 90 % for one robot.

Limits:
  * The 39 houses are the rectangular-room part of HouseExpo, not a random sample, and open-plan openings were
    narrowed to 1 m doors.
  * There is one seed per house.
  * Headless has no rendered camera.
  * Nothing here is a like-for-like comparison with published exploration numbers (see above).
