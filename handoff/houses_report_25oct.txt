Smoke Rescue houses report, 25 October 2026.
I built two furnished single-floor ranch layouts, their fire configurations and 600-second full-mission scenarios on houses-25oct. Family ranch has nine rooms and a 15 by 11 m footprint, 165 m2 gross. Long ranch has a central hallway and ten named rooms in an 18 by 11 m footprint, 198 m2 gross. The brief's long-ranch room list adds up to eleven spaces when the hallway is counted. I retained all named spaces, including four bedrooms and a separate family room.
Each plan has one entrance, 1 m clear interior openings, a 1.6 m double front entrance, a 1.75 m hallway, five people in different rooms and one rear-bedroom bed fire origin. The scenarios retain the seed 934 full mission's 600-second cap, 480-second search deadline, 320-second fire head start, smoke densities, dark rooms adapted to the new IDs, local launch pads and B placement error. Both living-to-hall cased openings are also 1.6 m. People use the existing s1, s2, s3, s4 and s6 posed assets. Their checked positions are fixed so a later scene load preserves their room and furniture clearance. All numeric config choices have sibling reason strings. No brain source or config/brain.json changed.
North is up in these sketches. Each D is a doorway to the hallway. The family entrance faces south through the living room. The long ranch also enters through its south living-room wall. The sketches show adjacency; the JSON has exact dimensions.

HouseMenu.Rebuild accepts a floorplan basename. Build House keeps its default floorplan; the two added Build House menu entries select the ranches. Selection survives an assembly reload during the editor session. Returning to Build House replaces variant furniture as well as geometry. RecorderMenu has the requested brain-only menu names and run IDs unity_family_ranch_full and unity_long_ranch_full. Each run rebuilds its selected house and furniture before people are placed. The recording, fire, fluid smoke and oracle floorplan_used.json now all load that scenario's selected plan. The recorder copies floorplan_used.json before the first frame and refreshes it at checkpoints. Default-house restoration runs when a variant recording finishes or its iterator fails. A running recorder blocks a house rebuild.
RanchExteriorBuilder generates a 22-degree pitched roof, 0.45 m eaves, fascia, gables, white siding cut around the openings, exterior sills, a flush porch slab and canopy, driveway, four lawn strips, shrubs, a tree and a mailbox. HouseBuilder supplies the framed glass windows. All materials already exist in Art/Materials. The exterior uses Unity primitives and generated gable meshes. Ground-level decoration stays outside the footprint and has no colliders. The original ground slab supports the robots. Roof parts sit above the ceilings on the cutaway layer. This preserves the evaluator's house-floor boundary. I did not open, launch or control Unity, import packages, download assets, or record a live run.
The small validator checks room tiling, grid alignment, nonoverlap, the single entrance, doorway boundaries and widths, graph reachability, wall and furniture clearance, 0.9 m paths from each door to its room centre, people clear of furniture and doors, distinct survivor rooms, valid fire fuel, mission settings and numeric reasons. The furniture placer uses rectangular approach checks and the existing 0.1 m flood fill. The random generator's enclosing-circle approximation rejects ordinary beds in these smaller rooms, so it is not the acceptance rule here. Existing random-layout generation code remains unchanged.
Four new C# tests exercise actual Scenario and FireSim loading, inheritance without early survivor placement, rejection of paths outside config and the default-house fallback. Those tests caught sibling smoke-density reason strings being parsed as room floats. Scenario.Load now excludes only the density map's reason annotations before typed deserialization. The file retains its annotations.
